# One round of budgeted Boolean composition

October 9, 2026. Starting tracked revision: `b7a801fa3`. The uncommitted Phase 3 files, as well as Phases 1 and 2, are preserved in an 85-file manifest.

**Outcome: one full binary-composition round is still insufficient.** There are small ordinary circuits that accept every binary Boolean combination of the Phase 3 noisy family, reject every GapMCSP NO instance in the stated fixed-beta regime, and still have no large accepting coordinate subcube. A further inexpensive XOR produces a missed YES instance.

This answers a bounded candidate question, not the full closure question. The new recognizer is complete for one round **from the old family**, not closed under all affordable compositions of its own accepted inputs. Full budgeted closure and the required general-circuit lower bound remain unproved. No novelty or P-versus-NP separation is claimed.

## The stronger requirement

Retain the Phase 3 definitions. An inner circuit has m address inputs; its N=2^m truth-table bits are the outer circuit's inputs. Ordinary gates are AND2, OR2, and NOT, with NOT counted and constants/input wires free.

Let J_k contain every k-junta on any support of at most k address coordinates. Let P be parity of all m address variables, and put

\[
\mathcal D_0=J_k\cup(P\oplus J_k),\qquad
\mathcal A_0=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal D_0}B_r(f).
\]

Here B_r is a truth-table Hamming ball. A full correct solver must accept cheap combinations of known YES functions whenever their **actual inner circuit cost** stays within s1. Merely accepting the original centers was not enough in Phase 3.

Define

\[
\mathcal D_1=\{\phi(f,g):f,g\in\mathcal D_0,
       \ \phi:\{0,1\}^2\to\{0,1\}\},\qquad
R=2r,\qquad
\mathcal A_1=\bigcup_{h\in\mathcal D_1}B_R(h).
\]

There are all 16 choices of phi, not just monotone operations. Supports may overlap, and the two operands may share computations. Since D0 is closed under complementation and contains constants, AND, NAND, and XOR of arbitrary D0 operands already generate D1: input complementation absorbs the remaining binary truth functions. The implementation checks this identity on all 16 truth tables.

Pointwise composition can change an output only where at least one argument changed. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{dist}(\phi(x,y),\phi(f,g))
\le\operatorname{dist}(x,f)+\operatorname{dist}(y,g).
\]

It follows that **every** phi(x,y) with x,y in A0 belongs to A1. Projection also gives A0 subset A1. The radius must double: two disjoint single-bit errors can produce two output errors.

This is a genuine extension of the old family, not only a composition of unperturbed centers. It includes the old missing parity on k+1 variables for k>=1, and includes AND(x0,x1) in the finite example where Phase 3 rejected it.

## Inner cost and explicit sharing

If the reachable gate-ID sets for two outputs are G and H, and a top construction adds q fresh reachable gates, their joined cost is at most

\[
|G\cup H|+q=|G|+|H|-|G\cap H|+q.
\]

For the literal AND, NAND, and XOR templates used in the shared-DAG experiment, all new gates and both argument cones reach the output, so equality holds. Projections or constants can discard an argument and only improve the bound. Gates with equal Boolean functions are not silently merged: the intersection counts the same actual nodes.

`SharedInner` expands the primitive DNFs, reuses input negations, computes shared full parity once, and memoizes explicitly identical atoms. For six address variables:

- Two distinct parity-shifted projection outputs each have 24 reachable gates but share 20. Their XOR uses 32 reachable gates, not 52.
- Reusing the same 24-gate output as both inputs to AND uses 25 reachable gates, not 49.

Allocated but unreachable gates are reported separately. The bounds below remain valid even with independent copies of both primitive circuits; they do not depend on assuming sharing that is absent.

Phase 3 supplies the primitive bound

\[
B_0=k+k2^k+4m.
\]

Every binary Boolean function of two existing wires uses at most four additional ordinary gates. `binary_gate` checks all 16 such templates. Thus every D1 center has an inner circuit of size at most `2B0+4`. Patching at most R addresses gives

\[
\operatorname{Size}(T)\le 2B_0+4+m+Rm+3\qquad(T\in\mathcal A_1),
\]

with zero changes needing no patch gates. The finite experiments build actual shared circuits and actual patched circuits, not just numerical size labels.

## Decoding composed centers

Fix a pair of k-element supports I,J, padding smaller supports if needed, and set U=I union J. A center generated by this pair depends only on

\[
(x_U,P(x)).
\]

These are features of an **address** x, not the outer input table. If |U|<m, each feature value has exactly

\[
L=2^{m-|U|-1}
\]

addresses: after fixing U, one still-unused coordinate can enforce the parity bit, and all other unused coordinates are free.

The outer circuit takes a majority within each feature fiber. If T is within R errors of a center on this support pair and `2R<L`, every majority recovers the center's feature value. Counting mismatches against those decoded values checks the actual distance.

It is crucial also to check that the decoded feature table belongs to D1. Accepting **every** function of 2k variables and parity would admit functions whose inner size can exceed the thresholds. The structural test below prevents that unsound enlargement.

## The structural test reduces to Boolean binary constraints

Write `K=I intersection J`, `A=I minus K`, and `B=J minus K`. Fix an assignment z to K. The unknown values of the two primitive functions are Boolean variables

\[
a_u\quad(u\in\{0,1\}^{A}),\qquad
b_v\quad(v\in\{0,1\}^{B}).
\]

For one fixed operation phi and fixed shift flags alpha, beta in {0,1}, the two decoded labels at a cell (u,v) must be

\[
\bigl(\phi(a_u,b_v),\ \phi(a_u\oplus\alpha,b_v\oplus\beta)\bigr).
\]

Each forbidden assignment `(a_u=a,b_v=b)` gives a clause

\[
(a_u\ne a)\ \lor\ (b_v\ne b).
\]

There are at most four clauses per cell. These are 2-CNF constraints. The usual implication-graph criterion tests satisfiability by checking that no literal and its complement are mutually reachable [S1, Theorem 1]. The source proves the satisfiability criterion; its linear-time RAM bound is **not** being treated as our Boolean gate count.

Different z assignments have independent primitive truth-table entries, so their constraints can be solved separately. But **phi, alpha, and beta must be the same for every z**. The circuit ANDs the slice tests for each fixed template before ORing the twelve choices: three canonical operations times four shift pairs. Choosing a different template per slice would be incorrect; a regression witness checks this distinction.

Satisfying values across all slices define actual functions on I and J. Thus the structural test is equivalent to the existence of two primitive functions with that template. It neither enumerates their exponentially many truth tables nor takes them as external witnesses.

### No SAT oracle in the outer circuit

The implication edges are ordinary Boolean signals derived from the decoded labels. Reachability is compiled by the recurrence

\[
Q^{j+1}_{uv}=Q^j_{uv}\lor(Q^j_{uj}\land Q^j_{jv}),
\]

starting with the active edges and the identity relation. Each update is one AND and one OR. Layers use only the preceding matrix, so this is an acyclic Boolean circuit even though the represented implication graph can contain cycles.

For q unknown Boolean variables, there are 2q literals. The literal construction uses `16q^3` gates for the matrix updates and `3q-1` for the final inconsistency checks. A separate reference implementation uses graph traversals, and small instances are independently checked by exhaustive assignments to the unknown primitive values.

## Quantitative outer size

Let `M=binom(m,k)`. There are M^2 ordered support pairs. For a pair with overlap h, the structural test has 2^h slices, each with

\[
q=2^{k-h+1}
\]

unknown Boolean values. Its total size is O(2^(3k)), uniformly in h. The fiber-majority and mismatch circuits cost O(N(m+1)). Consequently the explicit ordinary network satisfies the conservative bound

\[
S\le4000M^2\bigl(N(m+1)+2^{3k}\bigr).
\]

`size_estimate` records exact counts by overlap, including all NOTs, counters, the twelve templates, slice conjunctions, support-pair disjunctions, and the distance tests. The compiler verifies its literal gate count against that formula.

Sharing is real in both parts: decoded fiber values feed multiple mismatch gates and constraint clauses, and reachability entries feed many subsequent matrix updates. Reachable wire fan-outs are recorded; the recognizer never counts a computed label as a freely fixed raw input bit. This is not a lower bound on the sharing of every equivalent circuit.

The recognizer computes exactly `1_{A1}` whenever

\[
2k<m,\qquad 2R<2^{m-2k-1}.
\]

Soundness follows from the structural witness and checked distance. Completeness follows by selecting the generating support pair and recovering its center by majority. The implementation rejects parameters outside this sufficient decoder regime rather than silently claiming completeness there.

## The original fixed beta regime is retained

Use the same parameters as Phase 3:

\[
s_1=\left\lfloor\frac{N^\beta}{cm}\right\rfloor,
\quad s_2=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor,
\quad k=\left\lfloor\log_2\frac{s_1}{8m}\right\rfloor,
\quad r=\left\lfloor\frac{s_1}{4m}\right\rfloor,
\quad R=2r.
\]

Fix `0<beta<1/5` and `c>=1`. For sufficiently large m, `m>=2`, `k<=m`, and `s1>=64m`. Since `B0<=5m+s1/8`,

\[
2B_0+4+m+Rm+3\le11m+\frac{3s_1}{4}+7\le s_1.
\]

Hence **every accepted table is a YES instance** in this fixed-positive-beta regime. In particular all NO instances, whose inner size is strictly greater than s2, are rejected. This is a sound recognizer for a strict subset of YES, not a full promise solver.

We still have `k=beta*m-2log_2(m)+O(1)` and `R=Theta(N^beta/m^2)`. The decoder's minimum fiber size is `Theta(N^(1-2beta)*m^4)`, so its radius condition holds eventually.

For distinct D1 centers, their two descriptions together use at most 4k raw address variables plus full parity. Once these features are fixed, a disagreement repeats on a fiber. If `4k<m`, this gives center separation at least

\[
\Delta=2^{m-4k-1}=\Theta(N^{1-4\beta}m^8).
\]

Because `1-4beta>beta`, eventually `Delta>2R+1`. The Phase 3 separated-tube theorem therefore applies: **every accepting coordinate subcube has dimension at most R**, including after any initial restriction. The minimum unrestricted input-certificate size is exactly N-R by the same ball argument.

Finally, `2^(3k)=o(N)` and `binom(m,k)<=2^(m H(k/m))`, so

\[
S\le N^{1+2H(\beta)+o(1)}.
\]

For any fixed epsilon>0, choose beta_0<1/5 so that `2H(beta)<epsilon` below beta_0. For each such fixed beta, the circuit eventually has size below `N^(1+epsilon)`. No vanishing beta(m) is substituted. The illustrative exact integer samples use c=1 but do not identify the universal constant in OPS; the argument works for any fixed c>=1.

Thus small outer size, NO soundness, and completeness for this **entire one-round noisy composition family** still do not yield the required certificate. Using the Phase 2 description bound `K(m,s)=(m+s+2)[16(m+s+2)^2]^s`, we have R smaller than s2 while `log_2 K(m,s2)>=4s2`. The Phase 2 counting contradiction therefore cannot be triggered.

## A further cheap composition still fails

For `m>=4k+2`, consider parity on a set J of 2k+1 address variables. Every D1 center depends on at most 2k raw coordinates U and full parity. There is a coordinate in J outside U and another in the complement of J outside U. Flipping both preserves U and full parity but toggles the target parity. Pairing addresses in this way proves its distance from **every** D1 center is N/2.

This missed table has a circuit of size at most 8k, well below s1 at the chosen lengths. It is already obtainable with two XOR steps from three primitive parities: split J into groups of sizes k, k, and one.

The first XOR, parity on 2k variables, is a D1 center. The remaining projection is also accepted. XORing them produces the rejected table despite its cheap shared-DAG witness. This explicitly distinguishes one-round completeness from closure under all affordable compositions of the **new** accepted set.

The finite m=6, k=1 witness is an actual four-gate XOR followed by another four-gate XOR. Its final eight-gate inner table is at distance 32 from every binary center and is rejected by the radius-two network.

## Decision after this bounded pass

There is a decisive **no-go result for the one-round strengthening**. There is still no positive inequality connecting the size of an arbitrary full GapMCSP solver to the desired raw-input certificate cost.

The next step should not automatically be another larger finite atlas. For three or more independently parameterized primitive functions, the analogous direct encoding can have clauses of arity three or higher, so the particular 2-SAT argument above no longer applies verbatim. That observation is **not** an NP-hardness proof for these structured instances; some higher-arity composition families remain tractable.

At the other extreme, starting from free input/constant wires and imposing affordable closure under all gates through budget s1 simply recreates the full YES set by topological circuit construction. That restatement is not a new structural lemma.

A continuation should first identify a genuinely resource-sensitive consequence of full YES completeness that is absent from this control, or reassess the certificate route. Full hardness magnification and the internal-oracle locality question remain as in the preceding phases.
