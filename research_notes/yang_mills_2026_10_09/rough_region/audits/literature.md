# Bounded original-source context: rough-region filling in lattice SU(2)

**Date:** October 9, 2026. **Scope:** five primary papers, not a survey of Yang–Mills or mass-gap status. This is context for the local replacement question in [the parent audit, §§3 and 5](../../audits/cutoff_bridge_obligations.md), not a discharge of its analytic obligations. No novelty or priority claim is made. The elementary distinctions below are self-contained; inaccessible literature is not a proof dependency.

**Bottom line.** Unrestricted interior **link** filling always exists for compatible fixed exterior link data. The substantive question is filling with specified curvature, boundary, holonomy/topological, measurability, and integrated-cost constraints. A continuum degree obstruction does **not** by itself rule out the unrestricted finite-link operation.

The accompanying discussion explicitly retains the assumption: “so we have Theorem 1 with the assumption.”

### [L] Lüscher: admissibility, cell gauges, and topological charge

M. Lüscher, *Topology of lattice gauge fields*, **Communications in Mathematical Physics 85** (1982), 39–48. [DOI/publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02029132); [inspected original-paper scan](https://scispace.com/pdf/topology-of-lattice-gauge-fields-1xfjc5u41i.pdf).

**Assumptions/results:** periodic four-dimensional hypercubic SU(2) links, period `L ≥ 2`. Section 1, pp. 39–40, (4) and properties (i)–(v), gives the sufficient condition

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(I-U_p)<0.03\quad\text{for every plaquette},
\]

with **unnormalized** fundamental trace. Such fields avoid the exceptional set; the constructed integer `Q` is constant along paths avoiding that set. The definition extends beyond the small-plaquette subset to non-exceptional fields.

**Concrete gauge passage:** §3, p. 43, (17)–(18), constructs coordinate-path cell gauges; §4, p. 46, explains why small plaquettes make their based-loop matrices near identity. This is cell-local, not boundary-fixed filling.

**Important counterweight:** p. 39 states that an unrestricted lattice field “can be continuously deformed to the trivial field.” Section 3's interpolation/cocycle construction explains where the extra topology enters.

**Not supplied:** a relative charge for an open block without additional gluing/boundary data, an admissible extension for every boundary, or a statistical replacement bound. Access succeeded through the browsing tool; direct HTTP retrieval returned 403.

### [U] Uhlenbeck: continuum small curvature, including the critical endpoint

K. K. Uhlenbeck, *Connections with Lp bounds on curvature*, **Communications in Mathematical Physics 83** (1982), 31–42. [DOI/publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01947069); [inspected original-paper text/scan mirror](https://www.scribd.com/document/992530813/Connections-With-L-p-Bounds-on-Curvature).

**Locators:** Theorem 1.3, p. 33; Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2, p. 34. A useful four-dimensional specialization: on the **trivial bundle over the unit ball**, compact structure group, `A ∈ W^{1,2}`, and sufficiently small `||F_A||_{L²}`, there is a `W^{2,2}` gauge with representative `a` satisfying

\[
d^*a=0,\qquad a(\nu)|_{\partial B^4}=0,\qquad
\|a\|_{W^{1,2}}\le C\|F_A\|_{L^2}.
\]

The endpoint is obtained by approximation/weak limits. By contrast, global compactness, Theorem 1.5/3.6, requires **`p > n/2`**. Page 32 warns about topology when gauge regularity no longer embeds continuously: “Care must be taken whenever the strict inequality does not hold.”

**Not supplied:** lattice interpolation estimates, an extension of an absent interior connection, or a gauge equal to identity on the boundary. The normal condition on `a` is not Dirichlet data for the gauge.

**Access:** inspected the original article body, not the mirror's AI-generated description; OCR is imperfect. No numerical smallness constant is extracted.

## 2. A concrete small-plaquette-to-gauge statement, independently checked

This elementary argument makes the cell-gauge mechanism in [L] explicit on a larger box; it is **not** presented as a verified version of [B-GF].

Let `B = {0,…,m}^d` with its full cubical edges and plaquettes, **free/nonperiodic geometry**, and unitary links satisfying

\[
\max_{p\subset B}\|I-U_p\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\varepsilon.
\]

For each vertex `x`, let `P_x` run from the origin to `x` in coordinate order `1,…,d`, and set `h_x = U(P_x)`. Then

\[
U^h_{x,x+e_i}=h_xU_{x,x+e_i}h_{x+e_i}^{-1}.
\]

Move the final `e_i` step of `P_x e_i` leftwards past the later-coordinate steps to obtain `P_{x+e_i}`. Each adjacent interchange contributes a transported plaquette (possibly inverted). There are at most `Σ_{j>i} x_j ≤ (d−1)m` such interchanges. Unitary conjugation preserves the norm, and telescoping products gives

\[
\boxed{\max_e\|I-U^h_e\|_{\mathrm{op}}
       \le (d-1)m\,\varepsilon.}
\]

The coordinate-tree links become identity. In four dimensions the bound is `3m ε`; a controlled logarithmic chart follows when this is sufficiently small. This construction is continuous in the finite link variables and requires no dimension-below-four hypothesis.

**Limits are essential:** the loss depends on the diameter in lattice units; the gauge need not be identity on the boundary; no plaquette energy is reduced by gauge transformation; and this constructs coordinates for an **existing** connection, not a new small-curvature interior.

For periodic geometry, take constant commuting links `U_1=V`, `U_j=I` for `j>1`, with `V^N≠I` on an `N`-periodic cycle. All plaquettes equal identity, but the cycle holonomy cannot be gauged to identity. Thus a global bound proportional only to plaquette curvature cannot hold without handling flat holonomy modes. This does **not** say that every near-identity representative is impossible.

For SU(2), direct diagonalization gives `Tr(I-U)=||I-U||²_op`; hence [L]'s `0.03` is an action-density threshold, not an operator-norm threshold of `0.03`.

## 3. What topology and the critical dimension actually restrict

The following are elementary deductions, separate from the imported theorem statements.

### Unrestricted finite-link filling is never obstructed by continuum degree

Choose one orientation for every free interior link. With compatible values already fixed on all other links, the configuration space is

\[
SU(2)^{E_{\mathrm{free}}}.
\]

It is nonempty (assign identity to free links) and path connected. Plaquettes computed from those links automatically satisfy the link-induced non-Abelian identities. One cannot instead prescribe all plaquette matrices independently. Mixed boundary plaquettes may become bad, and the action need not be small: existence alone is almost vacuous for the intended comparison.

A continuous map `g:S³→SU(2)≃S³` of nonzero degree cannot extend continuously over `B⁴`, because any such extension would make it null-homotopic. But boundary link values are **not** a specified continuous group-valued map on the entire boundary sphere. Even values of a gauge transformation at finitely many vertices can always be extended to interior vertices.

An especially direct example: if prescribed boundary links have the pure-gauge form `U_xy=g_x^{-1}g_y`, extend the **vertex values** `g_x` arbitrarily to interior vertices and use that formula on every remaining link. Every plaquette then telescopes to identity. This finite flat extension need not continuously interpolate the original boundary map. Therefore even the word “flat” does not restore the continuum degree argument without the missing interpolation requirements.

The obstruction concerns an extension preserving a specified continuous interpolation/trivialization or related constrained class. It does not even forbid every **curved continuum connection** with a prescribed boundary connection: extending a Lie-algebra-valued connection form is different from extending its pure-gauge parametrizing map. These distinctions must precede any proposed degree-based no-filling argument.

Likewise, an admissible-sector obstruction is not an obstruction to the full product of link groups. A path between different well-defined charges must leave the domain where that integer is continuous; unrestricted link surgery is allowed to do so. If an algorithm demands sector preservation, admissibility, or a particular boundary gluing, these are **additional constraints** to state and prove compatible, not automatic properties of lattice links. See [L] for the precise exceptional-set distinction.

### Four dimensions are critical analytically, not for assigning finite links

Under `A_r(x)=r^{-1}A(x/r)`, curvature scales as `F_r(x)=r^{-2}F(x/r)`, so

\[
\|F_r\|_{L^p(B_r^d)}=r^{d/p-2}\|F\|_{L^p(B_1^d)}.
\]

Thus `L²` curvature is scale invariant in `d=4`. Shrinking a rescaled concentration does not make its energy small. For a **fixed** integrable curvature, restriction to sufficiently small balls does make the integral small; these are different assertions, and the latter need not give a uniform radius for a family.

Moreover, a plaquette supremum bound yields only `Σ_p ||I-U_p||² ≤ |P| ε²`, not a volume-independent small integrated norm. Applying [U] requires an actual continuum connection and a justified interpolation/norm comparison. At its critical endpoint, `W^{2,2}(B⁴)` does not embed into continuous functions, so a Sobolev gauge must not be treated automatically as a continuous extension of a prescribed degree-carrying boundary map. Neither issue invalidates the finite-box algebra in §2.

## 4. Requirements not discharged by these citations

No inspected result supplies the whole following package. These are requirements to check against the self-contained local proofs, not an assertion that every item remains unproved there. For the parent's `t=H^{-1/2}(log H)^P`:

1. **Boundary-data class and support:** which exterior/collar links remain fixed; how crossing plaquettes and gauge covariance are handled; which holonomies or well-defined gluing/sector labels are retained.
2. **Constrained existence:** that the desired output class is nonempty for those data, with no unrecorded newly bad boundary plaquettes. If not, record the obstruction and its cost rather than dropping the input.
3. **A measurable replacement law and entropy bound:** density at most `exp(C n log H)` in the stipulated Haar reference measure. Existence or uniqueness of a minimizer does not give this: a deterministic point choice is singular with respect to product Haar measure when links are free.
4. **Integrated energy gain and summability:** the intended `exp[-c H t² n/(log H)^C]` gain and support-hit bounds, with declared geometric and boundary uniformities. Gauge fixing alone leaves the Wilson action unchanged.

**Stopping point:** [B-LF] is verified regional context; [B-GF] and [B-BG] remain carefully limited original-source pointers; [L] and [U] distinguish admissibility and critical regularity from unrestricted filling. These five papers neither certify the parent's quantitative replacement estimate nor establish that it is impossible. No further literature search is needed for the self-contained local arguments on that basis.
