## 2. Independent verification of the proposed 4D algebra

Write `F = log Z`, let `D_i` double coordinate `i`, and set

\[
p_i(x)=2F(x)-F(D_i x),\qquad
\delta=D(r)=16F(r,r,r,r)-F(2r,2r,2r,2r).
\]

All the following use one common `F`, nonnegative `p_i`, and own-direction squaring only.

### Coordinate bookkeeping

Telescoping with direction `i` last gives

\[
p_i(2r,\ldots,r,\ldots,2r)\le\delta.
\]

The proposed unsquared and squared boxes are exactly:

| `i` | Box defining `a_i` | Box defining `u_i` |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `(r,2r,2r,2r)` | `(2r,2r,2r,2r)` |
| 2 | `(4r,r,2r,2r)` | `(4r,2r,2r,2r)` |
| 3 | `(4r,4r,r,2r)` | `(4r,4r,2r,2r)` |
| 4 | `(4r,4r,4r,r)` | `(4r,4r,4r,2r)` |

Starting at the opposite-face box, double coordinates `j = 1,...,i-1` in ascending order. Immediately before step `j`, earlier coordinates are `4r`, coordinate `i` is `r`, and all other coordinates are `2r`. Consequently `D_i x` is **exactly** the box defining `u_j`. There is no incorrect replacement by a term from another shape.

The exact identity is

\[
p_i(D_jx)=2p_i(x)-2p_j(x)+p_j(D_ix).
\]

Dropping the nonpositive term and iterating therefore gives precisely

\[
a_i\le 2^{i-1}\delta+
\sum_{j<i}2^{i-1-j}u_j.
\]

The ascending order matters to this identification. No transverse-volume monotonicity is assumed or proved.

### Constants and iteration

At `delta <= 1/2048`, the successive bounds are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
i&a_i\text{ upper bound}&u_i\text{ upper bound}\\\hline
1&\delta&4\delta^2\\
2&2\delta+4\delta^2\le3\delta&36\delta^2\\
3&4\delta+44\delta^2\le5\delta&100\delta^2\\
4&8\delta+188\delta^2\le9\delta&324\delta^2.
\end{array}
\]

In particular every squaring input is at most `9/2048 < 1/4`. The weighted sum is

\[
8\cdot4+4\cdot36+2\cdot100+324=700.
\]

Thus `D(2r) <= 700 D(r)^2 <= 1024 D(r)^2`. The latter recurrence preserves the threshold, since `1024/2048 = 1/2`, and yields

\[
\delta_k\le 2^{-10}(1024\delta_0)^{2^k}
\le 2^{-10}2^{-2^k}.
\]

At the cube of side `w_k = 2^k n`, choosing the desired transfer direction first in the telescoping order gives `8p_i <= delta_k`. Therefore

\[
(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^{w_k}\le2p_i
\le\delta_k/4\le2^{-12}2^{-2^k},
\]

which implies the stated ratio `lambda_2/lambda_1 <= 2^(-1/n)`. The zero-defect/rank-one case is included.

**Independent computation performed:** represented expressions as exact integer linear combinations of formal `F(x)` values; checked all 24 telescoping orders, the final weighted defect, and all four preparation identities **including the discarded terms**. Checked the scalar inequalities with exact rational arithmetic. At the threshold, the respective preparation ratios are bounded by `1`, `1025/512`, `2059/512`, and `4143/512`, below the proposed caps `1,3,5,9`. All passed. No floating-point sampling was used for these algebraic checks.

### Gluing and local limits

The local-limit argument retains the stronger estimate

\[
p_1(w_k/2,w_k,w_k,w_k)\le\delta_{k-1},\qquad
s_{w_k/2}(w_k)\le e^{\delta_{k-1}}-1\longrightarrow0.
\]

For a fixed slab of thickness at most `w_k/2`, separating the ground projection in the numerator and denominator gives the stated error `2 ||A||_infinity s_{w_k/2}`. The insertion-norm assumption supplies the cylinder covariance bound with no volume prefactor. These are the needed ingredients; an eigenvalue-ratio bound alone would not replace the trace-tail estimate, as counterexample C6 below shows.

The parent cutoff-comparison proposition also has the correct factor:

Its common bulk subtraction cancels. Its two quantitative premises remain separate, explicitly unproved inputs; the proposition does not produce them.

## 3. SU(2) strong-coupling check and the zero-coupling endpoint

For the specified convention

\[
Z_\beta(x)=\int\exp\left[\frac\beta2\sum_p\operatorname{Tr}U_p\right]
\prod_e dU_e,\qquad \int dg=1,
\]

there are `6 V` plaquettes. With every period at least four, each plaquette uses distinct independent links under the product-Haar reference law, so its trace has mean zero. Also `Tr U_p/2 <= 1`. Jensen's inequality and the pointwise upper bound give

\[
0\le F_\beta(x)\le6\beta V.
\]

Hence, using the lower bound on the **larger** box,

\[
D_\beta(4)=16F_\beta(4^4)-F_\beta(8^4)
\le16\cdot6\beta\cdot4^4=24576\beta.
\]

At `beta = 2^(-26)` this is exactly `3/8192`, with margin `1/8192` below the threshold `1/2048 = 4/8192`. Nonnegativity of `D`, needed elsewhere, comes from the transfer traces, not from these separate bounds on `F`.

This is a valid, deliberately tiny **fixed-lattice strong-coupling application**, consistent with the established strong-coupling framework [P2]. It supplies no weak-coupling trajectory estimate.

At `beta = 0`, all the normalized-Haar partition functions equal one, and the physical transfer operator is the projection onto constants. There is an important distinction:

- On the full gauge-invariant time-slice `L2` space, the vacuum complement need **not** vanish: the transfer operator annihilates it. The extended transfer gap `-log 0` is infinite.
- A link-reflection quotient, or restriction to surviving finite-energy states, can leave only the vacuum.
- A noninjective rank-one transfer operator on a larger space is not `exp(-H)` for a finite self-adjoint Hamiltonian on that whole space. One must not silently take a finite logarithm at its zero eigenvalues.

Thus the successful test does not establish nontrivial finite-mass excitations. The final parent text explicitly disclaims that implication and records the time-slice/quotient distinction.

## 4. Normalization tests: bulk terms are not arbitrary constants

For a replacement `F'(x) = F(x) + h(x)`,

\[
p_i'(x)-p_i(x)=2h(x)-h(D_ix),\qquad
D'(r)-D(r)=16h(r^4)-h((2r)^4).
\]

Consequences:

1. **A common extensive term is harmless.** For `h(x) = c product_i x_i`, both changes vanish. This covers the usual Wilson action shift by `beta` per plaquette, or a fixed normalization per link/plaquette on these periodic lattices.
2. **A constant per connected torus is not harmless.** Multiplication by `C` changes each directional defect by `log C` and the 4D defect by `15 log C`. It leaves all normalized expectations unchanged. Equality of normalized Gibbs measures therefore does not justify substituting their partition functions in this criterion.
3. **Topology/shape factors require accounting.** A degeneracy, a sector average, a residual group-volume convention, or a nonextensive determinant may contribute such a term. This does not assert that a properly constructed probability-Haar SU(2) trace has an extra factor; the interface explicitly avoids one.
4. **Subtracting the finite-volume vacuum energy is not generally a bulk subtraction.** Dividing `Z(t,W)` by `lambda_1(W)^t` preserves the defect in direction `t`, but need not preserve defects in transverse directions or the common all-direction trace representation. A nonextensive Casimir contribution cannot be discarded as though it were `c V`.
5. **A free-energy-density estimate is not the absolute defect estimate.** If errors in the two logarithms are bounded by `e_r,e_2r`, the defect error can be `16e_r+e_2r`. A per-volume error `epsilon` alone allows an error of order `r^4 epsilon`. Exact common bulk cancellation or a stronger residual estimate is required.

**C1 — topology normalization destroys the conclusion.** Let `K = I_g`, `g >= 2`, for every cross-section, and `Z(x) = g`. Genuine traces have `p_i = log g` and `D = 15 log g`. There is zero gap above any selected vacuum. If one divides out the “topological factor” `g`, all defects become zero although the original spectrum is unchanged. Alternatively a fixed multiplier `C = exp(-D(r)/15)` can force a zero defect at any chosen scale. Such a modified partition function is not licensed by the exact full-trace assumption. The example deliberately drops that assumption; it is **not** a counterexample to the parent theorem.

The same example shows why `D(r)/r^4 -> 0` is insufficient: a zero-gap vacuum multiplicity contributes only a constant to `D`.

## 5. Spectral and positivity counterchecks

### Degeneracy and multiplicity cannot hide in a genuine positive trace

For normalized eigenvalues `1, exp(-E_1),...`, let

\[
s_t=\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-tE_j}.
\]

The purity argument gives `s_t <= exp(p_t)-1`, counting **every** multiplicity. In particular a vacuum multiplicity `g` gives `p_t >= log g`. A small test forbids that multiplicity; positivity-improving kernels are a convenient independent way to obtain simplicity, not a mechanism for making multiplicities disappear.

For a single extra level of energy `epsilon`,

\[
p_t=2\log(1+e^{-t\epsilon})-\log(1+e^{-2t\epsilon})
\longrightarrow\log2\quad(t\epsilon\to0).
\]

This is a useful exact regression: an included low-energy state cannot be diluted away by invoking its small overlap with one observable.

### Positive Boltzmann entries are not operator positivity

**C2 — strictly positive entries with a negative eigenvalue.** For `0 < a < 1`,

\[
K_a=\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\1&a\end{pmatrix}
\]

has eigenvalues `a+1` and `a-1`. Nevertheless every entry is strictly positive. Its one-step defect is

\[
p_1=\log\frac{2a^2}{1+a^2}<0.
\]

Distinguish (i) positivity-preserving kernels, used for domination and the Perron vector; (ii) positive-semidefinite operators, used for nonnegative spectral weights; and (iii) the reflection-positive reconstruction and its positive one-step translation. The original O(4) source proves the extra operator property; the Wilson transfer construction has its own proof [P1].

**Qualification:** this example does not break the parent's even-length recursion. Even powers of a self-adjoint operator have nonnegative eigenvalue weights and can control the **absolute** eigenvalue ratio. What fails without the additional positivity argument is the stated positive one-step physical transfer interpretation. A two-step repair must retain its time-unit conversion.

### A selected observable is not the full spectrum

**C3 — a fast observable misses a slow physical state.** On a three-dimensional Hilbert space let

\[
H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\epsilon),\qquad
\Omega=e_0,\qquad A=|0\rangle\langle1|+|1\rangle\langle0|.
\]

Then `⟨Omega,A exp(-tH) A Omega⟩ = exp(-t)` while the full gap is `epsilon`. Taking `epsilon -> 0` invalidates inference from this channel to a uniform full gap.

The parent avoids this problem twice: its finite-volume trace counts all included states, and its limiting spectral exclusion is applied on a dense local-observable domain. Constants depending on an individual observable do not obstruct spectral exclusion if the exponential rate is common. Density, not a single favored glueball/plaquette channel, is the essential point.

## 6. Gauge projection, flux sectors, and twisted traces

Gauss-law projection is necessary and is not an illicit omission of physical states. With probability Haar,

\[
P_G=\int R(g)\,dg,\qquad P_G^2=P_G.
\]

One must retain the trace on its invariant range with the induced inner product. Normalized averaging already handles the kernel and stabilizers of the gauge action; a second, unspecified division by an orbit volume is not justified. The explicit interface in the repository makes the correct distinction between unprojected and physical transfer spaces [P1].

For center electric-flux sectors on a spatial torus, write algebraically

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm phys}=\bigoplus_e\mathcal H_e,
\quad Z_e(t)=\Tr_{\mathcal H_e}e^{-tH},
\quad Z_{\rm tw}(k,t)=\Tr(U(k)e^{-tH})
=\sum_e\chi_e(k)Z_e(t).
\]

The inverse finite Fourier transform projects onto `e`; the untwisted trace `k = 0` **sums** over these electric sectors rather than selecting zero electric flux. The distinction between temporal twists, electric-flux projections, and fixed spatial magnetic twists is explicit in [P4]. The equations above are the elementary trace consequences of a commuting finite symmetry, not a claim based on the numerical results of that paper.

**C4 — a positive number called a twisted partition function need not be a purity trace.** Let `T = diag(1,r)`, `0 < r < 1`, and `U = diag(1,-1)`. Then

\[
Z_{\rm tw}(t)=1-r^t>0,\qquad
2\log Z_{\rm tw}(t)-\log Z_{\rm tw}(2t)
=\log\frac{1-r^t}{1+r^t}<0.
\]

This remains a problem at even `t`: the fixed twist is inserted **once** in both traces, not raised to the corresponding power. A classical twisted path integral can have positive Boltzmann weights while having this character-weighted spectral interpretation.

Likewise `Tr(P_e T^t)` is a positive trace on a **restricted** Hilbert space; it cannot bound omitted physical sectors. For example projection onto the vacuum in C3 gives a constant trace one while a low level remains elsewhere.

Scope checks:

- For the untwisted periodic SU(2) problem, count all states in that specified physical Hilbert space, including its electric-flux sectors. Do not silently replace the trace by a sector ratio or an average over twists.
- A different spatial magnetic twist, global gauge-group form, or external-charge sector is a different boundary/representation problem; this theorem does not automatically cover it.
- Four-dimensional instanton charge is a history label, not automatically a conserved time-slice sector. A fixed-charge restriction needs a separate gluing argument. A theta weight introduces `exp(i theta Q)` and does not inherit the positive-weight proof automatically.

An included low-flux state on a *later dyadic cube* would increase that cube's trace tail and contradict the proved estimate. Thus “new flux states may emerge” is not a counterexample once the full-trace and rectangle hypotheses really hold at every required box.

## 7. Volume and thermodynamic-state quantifiers

The following examples identify missing assumptions in stronger slogans; none supplies a common all-direction torus model satisfying the parent's small-defect hypothesis.

**C5 — fixed-volume ground dominance does not imply a uniform gap.** Define

\[
T_L=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+e^{-1/L}&1-e^{-1/L}\\
1-e^{-1/L}&1+e^{-1/L}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is positive definite with strictly positive entries and simple top eigenvalue one. Its gap is `1/L`. At each fixed `L`, `p(t,L) -> 0` as `t -> infinity`; even along `t = L^2`, the defect tends to zero. There is still no volume-uniform gap. This abstract family intentionally lacks the cross-direction trace relations used by the 4D theorem.

**Dyadic versus arbitrary sizes.** The same matrices with gap `1` when `L = 2^k n` and gap `1/L` at other integer widths give an elementary logical separation between a dyadic bound and an all-width bound. This is not advertised as a homogeneous local lattice model. Additional locality/comparison arguments might extend a particular theorem, but the displayed doubling recursion does not itself supply them. The parent makes only the justified dyadic claim.

**C6 — a uniform gap alone does not identify cubic tori with ground-state cylinders.** Let a transfer operator have eigenvalue one and `N_L = ceil(exp(L^3))` eigenvalues equal to `exp(-1)`. Its gap is uniformly one, but at temporal extent `L`,

\[
s_L=N_Le^{-L}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

This is an abstract spectral example, not a claim about a local Wilson action. It shows why the actual trace-tail estimate is doing extra work. In ordinary independent gapped local systems the entropy is much better controlled, but such control is not a consequence of the numerical gap alone. The parent uses the correct stronger tail estimate.

**C7 — pure-state clustering does not imply clustering of a periodic mixture.** Consider the exact nearest-neighbor constraint that all Ising variables agree. Its two deterministic states have trivial centered local Hilbert spaces. Their equal mixture has `E sigma = 0` and `E sigma_x sigma_y = 1` at every separation, hence a zero-gap local vector. The finite-torus partition function is two, so the genuine defect is `15 log 2`, not small. The example separates a statement in a selected state from uniqueness or a statement about every mixture.

**C8 — a local bulk gap is not a finite-torus gap above one vacuum.** Kitaev's toric-code Hamiltonian has four ground states on a torus and a positive gap above their joint ground space [P5]. Its gap on the complement of a **single** vacuum is zero. On the plane the corresponding bulk vacuum need not carry that torus multiplicity. This is not a 4D SU(2) counterexample; it cleanly separates the two meanings of “full gap.” Dividing the torus multiplicity out would repeat C1's error.

Consequently:

- “Uniform” here means uniform in the specified dyadic cross-section index at a fixed cutoff/coupling.
- The local-limit full gap concerns the cyclic vacuum Hilbert space of the admitted local physical algebra.
- Global winding states whose supports grow with the torus need not survive as vectors of that limiting local representation.
- A different thermodynamic state or an externally charged representation is not covered merely by calling the result “full.”

## 8. U(1) Coulomb phase: the appropriate negative control

The useful control is **weak-coupling, four-dimensional** compact U(1), not U(1) at all couplings. Fröhlich–Spencer establish a massless phase; the proof text inspected gives the explicit field-strength masslessness argument for the Villain formulation, using nonsummable plaquette correlations [P3]. This audit does not silently upgrade that proof to a new precise Wilson-action theorem, nor assume identification of every possible thermodynamic state.

Why this matters: compactness, gauge invariance, nonnegative Boltzmann weights, and positive transfer operators do not by themselves supply the **small-defect antecedent** in a Coulomb phase. The abstract conditional criterion is compatible with U(1); a proposed weak-coupling proof of its antecedent using only properties shared by the Coulomb model needs another examination. To obtain a literal contradiction with a particular periodic-limit claim, match the action, boundary conditions, and state of the masslessness result.

### Exact free-Maxwell calibration

A particularly transparent quantitative test is free Maxwell theory on a spatial cubic torus of physical side `L`, with photon speed one. Its nonzero momentum modes have

\[
E_{\mathbf m}=2\pi|\mathbf m|/L,
\qquad \mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\},
\]

and two transverse polarizations. The canonical torus partition function includes oscillator and zero-mode contributions [P6, equations (4.26)–(4.30)]. In particular even the zero-flux sector retains physical photon modes; removing global fluxes does not remove the Coulomb obstruction.

At temporal extent `t = L`, **one** photon state already gives `s_L >= exp(-2 pi)`. Whenever the complete torus trace obeys the all-direction positivity assumptions,

\[
D(L)\ge8p_{\rm time}(L)
\ge8\log(1+s_L)
\ge8\log(1+e^{-2\pi})\approx0.0149256,
\]

well above `1/2048`. Counting the twelve lowest one-photon states improves this lower bound to approximately `0.1772953`. These numbers are calculated here from the free spectrum; they are **not** asserted as exact bounds for compact Wilson U(1).

Zero modes provide another check. In the convention

\[
H=\frac{g^2}{2}\int\Pi^2+\frac1{2g^2}\int B^2,
\]

a compact holonomy of period `2 pi` has rotor energies `g^2 |n|^2/(2L)`, and magnetic fluxes `2 pi m` have energies `2 pi^2 |m|^2/(g^2 L)`. Both scale as `1/L`. These follow respectively from the holonomy inertia `L/g^2` and the constant magnetic field `2 pi m/L^2`; conventions change coefficients, not the scale. A computation that removes these contributions, the photons, or the finite-size vacuum term must no longer claim to be evaluating the same full trace.

The test distinguishes two issues: it rejects a false universal *smallness estimate*, not the positive-trace conditional algebra.

## 9. Exactly what the conditional SU(2) result implies

With the verified transfer interface and the antecedent at one even `n`, the finite-lattice conclusion is a bound on every eigenvalue in the physical trace's vacuum complement on spatial cubes `(2^k n)^3`. With the final canonical local-insertion/reconstruction interface and its explicit weak-limit approximation steps, every selected dyadic periodic local limit has the same full local-physical transfer bound.

That is already the bounded claim in the parent file. It does not assert:

- all rectangular volumes, all boundary conditions, or thermodynamic-state uniqueness;
- smallness of the defect on an asymptotically free weak-bare-coupling trajectory;
- a nontrivial continuum theory or convergence of its observables;
- an isolated particle, a finite quantitative upper mass bound, confinement, or a statement in every magnetic/charged/theta sector.

For a future cutoff application, a positive physical lower bound would require control of `a n(a)`, because

\[
m_{\rm phys}(a)\ge\frac{\log2}{a n(a)}.
\]

**C9 — positive cutoff gaps can collapse in physical units.** Eigenvalue ratio `exp(-a^2)` gives lattice gap `a^2 > 0` but physical gap `a -> 0`.

**C10 — a successful positive lower bound can coexist with a trivial limit.** A fixed lattice ratio `exp(-1)` has physical excitation energy `1/a -> infinity`. Its finite-energy continuum sector can be only the vacuum. The rank-one `beta = 0` test is the sharp endpoint version.

Passing a uniform physical lower bound to a limiting full spectrum needs convergence of normalized semigroup matrix elements on a dense limiting domain (or the positive-time-regularized version in the starting source's `physical.tex`). Allowing an extra, unapproximated low-energy Hilbert summand defeats any assertion based only on a nondense set of convergent observables. The parent does not claim such a passage without additional input.

For orientation only, the official Yang–Mills problem asks for a nontrivial continuum theory with the specified axiomatic properties and finite positive mass scale, for compact simple gauge groups; it lists confinement and an isolated particle as further questions [P7, sections 3–5]. The present conditional lattice theorem is not a solution of that problem, nor does it claim to be.
